Data Protection · October 2026

KVKK draws a line on employer access to corporate email

The Turkish Data Protection Authority's Principle Decision 2026/2035 confirms that owning the tool does not grant free access to its content. Monitoring must be proportionate, announced in advance and documented.

So what: technical access is not legal access. Review monitoring policies, employee notices and investigation playbooks before the next internal inquiry, not during it.

Full analysis in Turkish · Issue 16 → AI Governance · October 2026

AI adoption in Turkey has doubled. Accountability has not kept pace.

Corporate AI use has moved from pilots into decision-making faster than ownership, oversight and audit trails have been defined. The bottleneck is no longer the technology.

So what: for every AI system that influences a decision, name an accountable owner and keep evidence of human oversight. That is what regulators and auditors will ask for first.

Full analysis in Turkish · Issue 15 → Cybersecurity · September 2026

The Cybersecurity Law's evidence test: obligations ahead of guidance

Turkey's Cybersecurity Law created duties for a broad set of organisations, while the secondary regulation that specifies controls and reporting is still being written. Companies are expected to comply with rules whose detail is not yet final.

So what: build to a recognised baseline such as ISO 27001 now and document the rationale. When the detailed rules arrive, a defensible record will matter more than a perfect guess.

Full analysis in Turkish · Issue 12 → Regulation · August 2026

Turkey's AI Action Plan is now in force by circular

The national AI Action Plan moved from policy document to implementation through a presidential circular, placing governance at the centre of public and private sector AI use.

So what: start with an inventory. An organisation that cannot list where AI is used cannot govern it, and an inventory is the first artefact any review will request.
